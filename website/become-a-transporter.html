Become a transporter

Truber is a freight marketplace for Zimbabwe. Transporters register their companies and trucks, then quote on loads that fit their routes.

Apply to join Open web app

Why transport with Truber

Truber gives transporters a clear place to find work, quote on terms they choose and keep a record of every trip.

  • Find loads that fit your trucks

    Browse loads by capacity, truck type, location and route, then choose which ones to quote.

  • Quote on your terms

    Send a quotation with your price and conditions for the trip.

  • Clear trip and delivery records

    Keep agreed terms, trip status and proof of delivery together in one record.

  • Ratings that stay with your account

    After a completed trip, ratings will stay with your account.

Your earnings

The shipper pays you directly. Truber charges you 5% commission on completed trips only.

How to join

Registration, documents and truck details will be reviewed before an account can take bookings.

  1. 1

    Register

    Create your transporter account and add your company details.

  2. 2

    Add company documents

    Add the documents Truber needs to verify your company.

  3. 3

    Register trucks

    Add each truck with its registration number, type, capacity, trailer specifications and documents.

  4. 4

  5. 5

Transporter questions

How do I get verified?

Register your company, then add your trucks and verification documents in the web app. Truber reviews them before you can quote or take bookings.

What documents do I need?

Company registration, an operator licence and an insurance certificate, plus the registration for each truck. You add them in the web app.

How much does Truber charge?

Truber charges 5% commission on completed trips only. There’s no fee to sign up or to quote.

Are payments protected?

No. Cash, bank transfer or mobile money are recorded on the trip but are Not protected. Truber doesn’t hold any funds.

How do I find loads?

Search posted loads in the web app and choose which ones to quote.
